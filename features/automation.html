What's included

We map what you do now, then build the parts that never vary.

01

The mapping comes first

An hour working out what actually happens to an enquiry today, including the steps nobody documented. This is where most of the value is, before anything is built.

02

Routing

Enquiries sorted by what they are and sent where they belong, instead of landing in one shared inbox that everybody can see and nobody owns.

03

Follow-up that does not rely on memory

A reminder fires when nothing has happened by the time you said it would. Quotes get chased the same way every time.

04

Appointment and enquiry workflows

Confirmations, reschedules and the handful of messages that have to go out in a set order.

05

Internal processes

The recurring admin that currently lives in one person’s head and stops entirely when they are on holiday.

What it deliberately doesn't do

  • We do not promise an integration before scoping it. What is possible depends on the tools you already run and what they expose.
  • We do not automate a process you have not agreed. If two people do it differently, that is a decision to make before it is a workflow.
  • We do not build something only we can maintain. You get a plain-language description of every step.
  • We do not automate customer conversations that should stay human.

Questions

The things people ask before saying yes.

Will this work with the software we already use?

Sometimes directly, sometimes with a workaround, occasionally not at all. We check before quoting rather than after, and we will tell you when the honest answer is no.

What if our process changes?

Then the workflow changes. They are built to be edited, and we document each step in plain language so a change is a conversation rather than an excavation.

Is this going to replace someone’s job?

In the businesses we work with it replaces the part of a job everybody dislikes — copying details between places and remembering to chase things.

Where do we start?

With whatever you have redone most times this month. That is almost always the right first automation.
