What's included

Set up around your business, not a generic script.

01

A briefing, in your words

Hours, services, coverage area, how you price, what you do not take on, and the answers you already give twenty times a week. You write it; we shape it into something it can say consistently.

02

A qualifying sequence

The three or four things you would want to know before agreeing to a job — asked every time, in the same order, so what you get back is comparable rather than a paragraph of guesswork.

03

Structured capture

Who called, what they need, how urgent it is, where they are, and the best number to reach them. Recorded the same way every time so it can be acted on without a call back to clarify.

04

Escalation rules you set

You decide what it must never handle alone. Anything matching those rules is taken down, flagged and routed to the right person rather than answered.

05

A consistent voice

It says what you would say. Most businesses find the briefing is the first time their own phone answers have ever been written down.

What it deliberately doesn't do

  • It does not improvise prices. If you have not given it a figure, it says a person will confirm.
  • It does not handle complaints, disputes or anything about work already done. Those route straight to you.
  • It does not pretend to be a person. Callers are told they are speaking to an assistant.
  • It does not connect to your calendar, CRM or phone system by default. Those are separate integrations we scope before promising them — see automation.

Questions

The things people ask before saying yes.

Will callers know it is not a person?

Yes, and that is deliberate. It introduces itself as an assistant. Pretending otherwise damages trust the moment somebody works it out, and people tend to be more forthcoming once they know what they are talking to.

What happens to a call it cannot handle?

It stops trying. It takes the caller’s details, tells them a person will call back, and flags the call according to the rules you set — so an unhappy customer reaches you rather than an FAQ.

Does it replace answering the phone?

No. It covers the calls that currently go to voicemail or ring out. Most businesses keep answering everything they can and use it for evenings, weekends and the hours when everyone is already on a job.

How long does it take to set up?

The build is quick; the briefing is the work. Most of the effort goes into writing down what you already say to callers.
